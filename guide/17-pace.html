A pace that works

The three weeks of Project 3 as I would pace them: what to do in each week and why in that order, the lectures that arrive while you work, how many hours each part takes, and why week 3 starts with the runs of final.

About 16 minutes to read.

On this page

  • How to use this page, and the three weeks at a glance
  • Week 1, week 2 and week 3, each with what to do and the lectures that land in it
  • Why this order: long runs early, and the reading and writing while they run
  • The lectures 20 to 24, and what each one adds to the project
  • How long it takes, for 498E and for 598E
  • The token load in week 3, and what to do if you fall behind

How to use this page

There are no checkpoints in Project 3, and nothing is checked on the dates below. Everything is due once, on Tuesday, November 24, at 11:59 pm. This page is the pace I would keep, so you can plan the three weeks around your other courses.

This page answers "when"; uv run p3 next answers "what now". On any day, it says where you are on the project's map and the one next thing to do, with the guide page that explains it, and the Guide runs it for you whenever you ask. The map on Start here has the same twelve stages, each marked with the week I would do it in.

The whole plan rests on one fact: a bench run goes on by itself in a plain terminal, a terminal with no Claude Code in it, while you do something else. In my pilot one full run took about 6 minutes for the baseline and about 17 for a config with review loops, plus its scoring; the baseline's three repeats took about 20 to 25 minutes, and three repeats of a larger config can take an hour or more; almost none of that is your time. So start long runs as early as you can, and do your reading and writing while they run.

Week 1: set up, the baseline and step 1

From the day P3 is released through Monday, November 9. On the map, that is stages 1 to 4.

  • On the first day: set up your repo (Set up your repo), open the Guide and the Builder (The Guide and the Builder), make the quick run of c0-baseline, and start its three full runs (Run the baseline). Setup is the step with the most ways to go wrong, and I would like it behind you while there is plenty of time.
  • While the baseline runs: read its quick run's outputs, KNOWN-GAPS.md and reviews/README.md, and write your PREFERENCES.md (Write your PREFERENCES.md). None of that needs a run, and the full runs use the snapshot in configs/c0-baseline/, so you can work in tutor/ while they go.
  • Then step 1 (Step 1: four skills): build the teach, tutor, quiz and grade skills in quick rounds, usually four rounds or more; snapshot c1-skills; make its full run; write its major review; then its section of EVAL.md and its entries in reviews/components.md.

The P4 proposal, due Friday, November 6, competes with this week. So start the baseline's runs on the first day, when they cost you a few minutes of attention, and do most of the rounds once the proposal is in, over the weekend and on Monday.

Try to finish the c1-skills major review this week. It is what week 2 is built from: the problems it names, with their item ids and quotes, become the checklists of your reviewers in step 2.

Week 2: review loops, the split and your test topics

Tuesday, November 10, to Monday, November 16. On the map, that is stages 5 to 8.

  • Step 2 (Step 2: review loops): reviewer subagents on teach, quiz and grade, added one per quick round, so at least three rounds, with checklists drawn from your c1-skills review; then the snapshot, the full run and the major review.
  • Step 3 (Step 3: split the teach job): the teach job split across subagents of your design, in quick rounds, then the snapshot, the full run and the major review, which covers the lesson only.
  • Your test topics (Your two test topics): write the items of both topics and commit them. They take about 1.75 hours each, they need no run, and they must be committed before their first run, which comes in week 3 once final is frozen. That makes them good work for the time a step's full run takes.
  • 598E, and 498E with the length-study stretch (Stretch options and the 598E length study): commit PREREG.md, then make the study's nine teach-only runs of c1-skills this week, since they need only c1-skills. PREREG.md has to be committed before the first of the study's eighteen teach-only runs, because git history is what proves the order.

Lecture 21 (Tuesday, November 10) brings the Agent SDK, and lecture 22 (Thursday, November 12) construct validity; The lectures below says what each adds. If you are in 498E and taking a stretch other than the length study, pick it early and try a small piece of it first, because most options draw on your Claude plan.

Week 3: final, its runs, and the writing

Tuesday, November 17, to Tuesday, November 24. On the map, that is stages 9 to 12.

  1. Freeze final with the guard, as early in the week as you can (final: the guard). final is what earned its place, plus a guard hook that keeps the answer keys from the tutor's main conversation, with its tests, and usually a grader subagent with a quick round of its own. Snapshot it, and commit tutor/.

  2. Start the runs that need it (final: the runs). uv run p3 next lists its three repeats, then the ablation, a final-minus-<x> snapshot (final with one component removed) and its one run, then the runs of c0-baseline and final on both test topics. Every one of them runs a frozen snapshot, so their order does not matter, as long as you bring tutor/ back with git restore tutor/ after the ablation's snapshot and before your learning sessions. For the length study, also the nine teach-only runs of final; try one 60-minute run first, because a teach scenario has 45 minutes at every length.

  3. Do the reading and writing while they run. Your three learning sessions with final and its major review; the blind comparison against c0-baseline, once final's three runs are scored; the open review of your test topics, once their runs are in; the repeats, read the way lecture 23 (Tuesday, November 17) reads them; the judge check, after lecture 24 (Thursday, November 19); the component inventory; EVAL.md; and the video.

Watch out

Hold the learning sessions on final as you snapshotted it. The ablation means taking one component out of tutor/ to snapshot it; git restore tutor/ brings final back, and all three sessions should be with final.

Write and commit the major review of final before you start the blind comparison, so your open judgment is on record before your blind one.

The deadline is two days before Thanksgiving, so plan to be done before the holiday traffic starts. Before you post on Canvas, run uv run p3 check --final and push your last commit (Hand in).

Why this order

  • Long runs first. A long run takes from a few minutes to more than an hour on the clock, and only a few minutes of your attention. A run started early leaves room for what can go wrong: a usage limit, a scenario that fails, a run you have to repeat. A run started on the last day leaves none.
  • Reading and writing while runs go. Most of your hours go to reading outputs, trying the tutor and writing about it, and much of that needs no new run. The blind comparison, p3 report, p3 check and p3 snapshot cost no tokens at all; the comparison's pairs come from runs you already have.
  • Each step is built on the one before. Your c2-review reviewers' checklists come from your c1-skills review; c3-split is measured against c2-review; final keeps what earned its place, which you only know once all three steps are measured.
  • Test topics in week 2. A test topic is committed before any run on it, so its items are on record before any result and nobody, you included, tunes the tutor to it. Its runs wait for final, so writing the items in week 2 means they are ready on the first day of week 3.
  • In week 3, the runs the most work depends on come first. The blind comparison needs three scored runs of final, and its major review reviews one of them; the test topics' open review needs their runs; the ablation is needed for one section of EVAL.md.

The token load in week 3

Every bench scenario draws on your own Claude plan, and on a subscription the cost is your usage limit, not dollars. Week 3 is when you are most likely to reach that limit.

Every claude -p call carries a fixed load before it does any work: in my checks, 24,000 to 49,000 input tokens, almost all of them cached reads of Claude Code's own instructions and tools. Every subagent pays a share of that load again, plus whatever it reads, so a tutor with many subagents uses up a weekly limit much faster than one without. In my pilot a full run of the baseline was 1.85 million tokens, and a full run of my review-loop config (four skills, with reviewers on teach, quiz and grade, at most two passes each) was 6.99 million. If your final costs about what my review-loop config did, its three runs are about 21 million tokens, and with the ablation and the test topics' runs that puts roughly 45 million tokens into week 3. That is why week 3 starts with final's runs: almost every token the project spends that week goes to them, everything else that week depends on them, and starting them first leaves you time if a usage limit arrives late in the week.

598E, plan for the cost

Your architecture is larger than my review-loop config, at least eight subagents where mine had six, and a 598E final may well take 10 to 15 million tokens per full run. You run it three times, on top of the ablation, the test topics and the length study's nine teach-only runs of final, three of them 60-minute lessons through your pipeline. Spread what can be spread: the baseline's runs in week 1, the length study's c1-skills runs in week 2 once PREREG.md is committed, and final's runs as early in week 3 as you can.

Some habits that keep the cost down, all through the project:

  • Check /usage in a Claude Code window before each big run.
  • Iterate on uv run p3 bench --config live --quick, and on --only with the one scenario you are changing: a round about the quiz needs --only quiz, not a teach scenario as well.
  • Try every new subagent, review loop or pipeline on an --only run before a full one.
  • Make full runs only of snapshots you mean to keep, and spread them over several days where you can.
If a usage limit stops a run

p3 bench stops at the first scenario that reports a usage limit, sets that repeat aside under bench/runs/_live/_aborted/, which git ignores, and keeps the repeats before it. Wait for the reset (/usage shows when) and run what is missing. In the meantime, do the work that needs no Claude: your test topics, your reviews of runs you already have, the blind comparison once its runs exist, and the write-up. When it breaks has the details.

If you fall behind

If you are well past the hours above by the end of week 2, come and talk to me early, while there is still time to plan around it. Office hours are on Tuesday and Thursday, 9 to 10 am and 2 to 3 pm, in CTLM 246L, or send me a message on Canvas. It is much easier to find a plan together in week 2 than on the last weekend.

Late work loses 20%, and nothing is accepted more than a week late (Hand in has the rules). And if something on this page seems wrong or unclear, tell me, because I would rather fix it than have you guess.
