Project 3 in one paragraph

You build a tutor that runs entirely inside Claude Code and does four jobs: it teaches a lesson, talks with you as a tutor, writes a quiz with its answer key, and grades your answers with feedback. The template ships a tutor that already does all four with a single skill, and you rebuild it in fixed steps, first four skills of your own, then review loops, then a split of the teach job into several subagents, then final, which keeps what earned its place, and you measure every step: what it bought in quality, and what it cost in tokens and time. Much of a tutor's quality is personal fit, and no script can tell you whether a lesson works for you, so you are the judge. At every step you read and try what your tutor made and write a review of it, and your reviews decide the next change. Fixed objective checks sit beside your reviews and catch what taste cannot, such as a wrong fact, a wrong answer key, or a quiz answer given away. The tutor itself is not the point; the engineering judgment is: which machinery pays for itself, which does not, and how you know.

What you build

Your tutor lives in tutor/ in your repo, and it does four jobs.

The four jobs, from the README's "What this project is".
JobWhat it does
Teachwrites a comprehensive lesson of a requested length, such as 30 minutes or an hour: a plan, explanations, examples, figures and visualizations, and external references where they help
Tutora conversational helper the learner talks to on the go, personalized to how the learner likes to talk, with guardrails: it does not hand out quiz answers
Quiza quiz with an answer key, at the right difficulty, covering the core of the lesson, clearly worded and aligned with the learning goals
Gradegrades plus useful feedback: a verdict on each answer and enough explanation, in the way this learner likes things explained

You build it as a fixed sequence of configs, which the README calls the ladder. A config is the tutor's instructions, preferences, skills, subagents and settings, frozen under a name with uv run p3 snapshot. The ladder is c0-baseline (the tutor as it ships), c1-skills (four skills of your own), c2-review (review loops on teach, quiz and grade, as subagents), c3-split (the teach job split across subagents), and final (what earned its place, plus a guard that keeps the answer keys from the tutor), with at least one final-minus-<x>, which is final with one component taken out. Your tutor, configs and snapshots explains what a config and a snapshot are, and The bench explains how each config is measured.

The map

The project runs through twelve stages, in this order. uv run p3 next uses the same numbers and names, so when it says "Now: 4 Step 1, c1-skills", you are on the fourth line below. The weeks are the pace I would keep; nothing is checked on those dates, and A pace that works says what fits in each week.

  1. Set upWeek 1

    Your own public copy of the template in work/p3-tutor, open in VS Code, with its CI check green, and the Guide and the Builder running in two terminals (The Guide and the Builder).

  2. The baselineWeek 1

    A quick run and three full runs of c0-baseline, the tutor as it ships, and a first read of what it made and of KNOWN-GAPS.md.

  3. Your preferencesWeek 1

    tutor/PREFERENCES.md rewritten in your own words, so every later review and the blind comparison measure fit to you.

  4. Step 1, c1-skillsWeek 1

    The four skills teach, tutor, quiz and grade built in quick rounds, then the snapshot, its full run, its major review, its section of EVAL.md and its inventory entries.

  5. Step 2, c2-reviewWeek 2

    Reviewer subagents on teach, quiz and grade, with checklists drawn from your c1-skills review, measured and reviewed the same way.

  6. The length study, part 1 (598E or length-study only)Week 2

    Sections 1 to 4 of PREREG.md, your plan, written and committed, then nine teach-only runs of c1-skills at 10, 30 and 60 minutes; if you are in 498E without this stretch, you skip this stage.

  7. Step 3, c3-splitWeek 2

    The teach job split across subagents of your design, measured on teach against c2-review.

  8. Test topicsWeek 2

    Two topics of your own in bench/own/, one from another Mines course and one life skill, written and committed before any run uses them.

  9. finalWeek 3

    What earned its place plus the guard hook, then its three runs, the ablation, the runs on your test topics, your three learning sessions and its major review.

  10. The blind comparisonWeek 3

    c0-baseline against final on all four jobs with the labels hidden, your picks, and the reveal.

  11. The write-upWeek 3

    Your open review of the test topics, the complete component inventory in reviews/components.md, and EVAL.md, with its judge check, with no TODO left.

  12. Hand inWeek 3

    uv run p3 check --final passing, then the video, the last push and one Canvas post, by Tuesday, November 24, at 11:59 pm.

Stages 4, 5 and 7 each run the same loop: quick rounds while you build, a snapshot, one full run, a major review, the step's section of EVAL.md, and the step's entries in the component inventory. Your reviews explains the quick rounds and the major reviews once, and each step page shows them for its own step.

The windows

You work on P3 in one VS Code window with your repo, work/p3-tutor, open as its folder, and in that window you keep a few terminals, each with one job. Two of them run Claude Code all the time: the Guide explains and points the way, and the Builder changes your tutor. In Project 2 one agent did both, and many of you told me that was confusing, so here they are separate.

  • Guide claude --agent p3-guide: explains what you are doing and why, and tells you the one next thing to do and which window does it; it never changes a file.
  • Builder claude: changes your tutor, one change per round, writes what it changed, and runs the p3 commands you ask for; it never plans the project.
  • Plain terminal a terminal with no Claude Code in it: runs the full bench runs, from about 6 minutes for one run of the baseline to an hour or more for three repeats of a larger config, which must keep going when a Claude Code window closes.
  • Your tutor claude started inside tutor/: the tutor you built, for learning with it.

The Guide and the Builder shows how to start each window, what to say to each, and what each will not do.

How to use this guide

The README.md in your repo is the brief: what you hand in, who owns which files, the ground rules, the grading and the deadline. This guide is the walkthrough: for every stage, what to do, in which window, and why the project asks for it. When the two ever disagree, the README wins, and I would like to hear about it.

  • Every step page opens with the short path, a box with four parts: Why the step exists, what to Do (three actions at most, each naming its window), how you know it is Done, and where to go Then. If you only want the next action, read the box. The sections below it explain how, with examples and the mistakes to avoid.

  • The exact words to type sit in boxes whose head names the window, with a copy button, like this one:

    Say to the Guide
    What is the one thing I should do next?

    The Guide checks your repo and answers with one next thing and the reason for it.

  • Commands sit in boxes whose head says where to run them, such as "Run in a plain terminal in work/p3-tutor", with what they print underneath when that helps.
  • Colored tags name the windows: Guide Builder Plain terminal Your tutor.
  • The Guide reads these same pages before it explains a step, and it names the page and section it used, so what the Guide tells you and what you read here agree.
  • The pages open from disk, with no server: open guide/index.html in your browser. Set up your repo shows the quickest ways.

Where am I?

Where am I?

Any time you are unsure, ask the Guide "What's next?", or run uv run p3 next yourself in a plain terminal in work/p3-tutor. It reads your repo, changes nothing, makes no model call, and prints where you are on the map above, the one next thing to do, and the page that explains it.

Run in a plain terminal in work/p3-tutor
uv run p3 next
p3 next: 498E (C:\...\work\p3-tutor)

Done:  1 Set up, 2 The baseline, 3 Your preferences
Now:   4 Step 1, c1-skills: quick rounds (1 of at least 2 complete)
Next:  In the Builder window, say: Act on Round 2 of reviews/c1-skills.md.
Read:  guide/08-step1-skills.html#rounds
Also:  the baseline's runs: 2 of 3 scored full runs (start the last one in a plain terminal)

That example is a student in the middle of step 1. This is how to read each line.

Done
The stages of the map you are past: every stage before the one on the Now line, and any later stage already complete.
Now
The stage you are on, and the item within it: the first stage with something not done. A stage whose only items left are long runs counts as passed once you have done something in a later stage, so a run that is still going never holds you back, and the runs left show under Also. When the next item needs runs you have not made yet, such as the blind comparison, which pairs only the runs there are, Now goes back to the first of those runs.
Next
The one next thing to do, with the window it happens in, or the exact command.
Read
The page of this guide, and the section, that explains that next thing.
Also
Shown only sometimes: a bench still running (a run folder with no run.json that changed in the last 90 minutes, which p3 next does not count as a failure, though p3 check does), at most two things left over from earlier stages, and, when the next thing is a long run, something you can do while it runs.

If uv run p3 check finds something broken, p3 next puts that first, because a broken repo comes before any next step, and it points you to When it breaks.
