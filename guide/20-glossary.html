Part IV · Reference

Glossary

Every term this guide uses, in alphabetical order, each defined in a sentence or two, with a link to the page that explains it in full.

About 21 minutes to read in full; look up the term you need.

On this page

Jump to a letter: A · B · C · E · F · G · H · I · J · K · L · M · O · P · Q · R · S · T · W · Y

Commands are written as p3 ...; you run each one as uv run p3 ... from work/p3-tutor, or as python -m p3 ... without uv.

The terms

ablation

final with one component removed, snapshotted under a name such as final-minus-quiz-review and run once on the shared topic, to see what that one component was worth. You need at least one. See final: the guard, repeats and the ablation.

agent_type

A field in the JSON that Claude Code hands a hook. Inside a subagent it holds the subagent's name, beside an agent_id; in the main conversation neither field is there, which is how your guard in final tells the grader from the main conversation. See final, under the guard.

allow list

permissions.allow in tutor/.claude/settings.json: the tools your tutor may use without asking. A bench run has no one to approve anything, so the bench passes this list to Claude Code, always adds Glob and Grep, and denies every other tool that would need a prompt, recording each denial in run.json. See Your tutor, configs and snapshots.

baseline

The tutor as the template ships it, snapshotted as c0-baseline: one teach skill doing all four jobs in one shot, with the neutral PREFERENCES.md. It is competent on purpose, because a broken baseline makes every change look like a win. See Run the baseline and read what it made.

bench, the

The measurement. p3 bench runs a config through the same scenarios from the same starting state, each in a fresh folder outside your repo, scores the outputs with fixed checks, and records what each scenario cost. See The bench: scenarios, scores and cost.

blind comparison

p3 compare: two configs' outputs for the same task, shown side by side as "Left" and "Right" without saying which is which, where for each pair you pick one or a tie, say why in a sentence, and say whether you could tell. Everyone compares c0-baseline with final on teach, quiz, grade and tutor; each session writes bench/compare/<session-id>.json and a key file, and you commit both. See The blind comparison.

Builder, the

Plain claude started at the root of work/p3-tutor, in its own terminal. It changes your tutor, one change per round, runs the p3 commands you ask for, and writes only the What changed part of each round and the inventory's factual fields; it never plans the project, and sends "what next" and "why" questions to the Guide. See The Guide and the Builder.

c0-baseline

The first config of the ladder: the baseline, as shipped. It gets three full runs on the shared topic, and one run on each test topic. See Run the baseline and read what it made.

c1-skills

Step 1: the four skills teach, tutor, quiz and grade, plus any others you like, and still one agent, so no subagent. It is also the first config with your own PREFERENCES.md. One full run. See Step 1: four skills.

c2-review

Step 2: review loops on teach, quiz and grade, with the reviewers as subagents; the tutor job gets none. One full run. See Step 2: review loops.

c3-split

Step 3: the teach job split across subagents of your design, such as a planner, a writer and a figure designer; tutor, quiz and grade are not split. Measured on teach, against c2-review. One full run. See Step 3: split the teach job.

component

A skill or a subagent that you built. Each one gets an entry in the component inventory, kept or dropped, and the section minimums count them. The guard is a hook, so it is not a component. See The inventory and EVAL.md.

component inventory

reviews/components.md: one entry per component, headed with its name, with six fields: Kind, Step added, Purpose, With and without, Review, and Kept in final. The Builder may fill in Kind, Step added, and With and without; Purpose, Review and Kept in final are yours. See The inventory and EVAL.md.

config

One version of your tutor: tutor/CLAUDE.md, tutor/PREFERENCES.md and everything under tutor/.claude/ except settings.local.json. The learner's files, such as lessons/ and keys/, are never part of it. A named config is a snapshot in configs/; live means tutor/ as it stands. See Your tutor, configs and snapshots.

contract (the output contract)

bench/FORMATS.md: exactly what the bench reads, the file names, numbering and JSON keys of lessons, quizzes, keys, grades and learning records, with a complete example of each. tutor/FORMATS.md is an identical copy for your tutor to follow, and you never change either. See Your tutor, configs and snapshots.

could tell

Your yes or no, for each blind pair, to whether you could tell which config made which. The reveal reports the share of pairs where you could, because blinding fails when two versions look obviously different. See The blind comparison.

EVAL.md

Your write-up, in thirteen sections from the baseline to the stretch: tables pasted from p3 report and from each reveal, pointers into reviews/, and your own prose about what each step bought and what it cost. p3 check --final fails while a TODO marker line is left in it. See The inventory and EVAL.md.

final

The last config: what earned its place from the steps before it (a step that did not pay may be dropped), plus your guard. It gets three full runs, at least one ablation, a run on each test topic, your three learning sessions, and the blind comparison against c0-baseline. See final: the guard, repeats and the ablation.

final-minus-<x>

The name of an ablation config, where <x> names what was removed. p3 check treats every config whose name starts with final-minus- as an ablation and asks for one scored full run of it. See final, under the ablation.

fixed files

The course's files that run the measurement, pinned by hashes in p3/bench.sha256: bench/FORMATS.md, bench/scenarios.json, everything under bench/judge/ and bench/topics/, and every .py file in p3/. p3 check fails when one changes, and you restore it from git. See Commands and files.

fork mode

A Claude Code setting, on by default, under which an interactive session cannot wait for a subagent's report. To learn with a tutor that uses subagents, turn it off in tutor/.claude/settings.local.json, which never reaches the bench. See When it breaks.

frontmatter

The block of settings between two --- lines at the very top of a SKILL.md or a subagent's file, the first of them on the first line. A subagent needs at least name and description, and a Markdown file in .claude/agents/ counts as a subagent only when its frontmatter has a name. See Your tutor, configs and snapshots.

full run

Every scenario of a topic, once: on the shared topic the ten scenarios teach, quiz, grade-s1, grade-s2 and tutor-p01 to tutor-p06. Only scored full runs count for the ladder, p3 report and the blind comparisons. One took about 6 minutes for the baseline and about 17 for a review-loop config in my pilot, and the teach scenario alone may run up to 45 minutes, so you start it in a plain terminal, where closing a Claude Code window cannot stop it. See The bench: scenarios, scores and cost.

grader

The subagent that final hands grading to, because once your guard is on, the main conversation can no longer read keys/; the guard lets the grader read them. A grader that cannot reach the key shows up as a fall in grade_verdict_agreement. See final, under the guard.

guard (two kinds, kept apart)

The Guide's guard is the course's: a PreToolUse hook registered in .claude/agents/p3-guide.md that lets the Guide read files and run only read-only commands; it runs .claude/hooks/guide_guard.sh, which hands each call to guide_guard.py. You never edit it. See The Guide and the Builder.

Your guard in final is yours to build: a PreToolUse hook in tutor/.claude/settings.json that keeps the answer keys in keys/ from your tutor's main conversation while a grader subagent can still read them, with tests of its own such as tests/test_guard.py. When a page about final says "the guard", it means this one. See final, under the guard.

Guide, the

Claude Code started with claude --agent p3-guide at the root of work/p3-tutor, in its own terminal; its startup header shows @p3-guide, and it sends its own first message. It explains each step and why it exists, names the one next thing to do, and hands off with the exact words for the Builder or the exact command for a plain terminal; it never changes a file. See The Guide and the Builder.

headline numbers

The twelve numbers p3 report and the reveal use: teach_facts_correct, teach_misconceptions, teach_length_ratio, teach_dead_links, quiz_key_agreement, quiz_wrong_key, quiz_ambiguous, grade_verdict_agreement, grade_false_wrong, grade_misconception_hit, tutor_leaks and tutor_behavior_met, plus the cost: total tokens, output tokens, minutes, subagents and the list-price equivalent. See The bench: scenarios, scores and cost.

hook

A command Claude Code runs at a fixed point in a session. A PreToolUse hook runs before each tool call and blocks it by exiting with code 2; in your tutor, a hook that fails in any other way, such as a script that is not found, lets the call through, unless its entry sets "onFailure": "block", which makes such a failure block the call instead. Hooks in tutor/.claude/settings.json run in the bench, and hooks in a subagent's frontmatter do not. See final, under the guard.

integrity failure

A FAIL line in the Integrity section of p3 check: something is broken, such as a changed fixed file, a snapshot that no longer matches its hash, or a run whose config changed. It fails p3 check and CI even without --final, and p3 next shows it before anything else. See When it breaks.

judge, the

A fixed prompt in bench/judge/ and a pinned model, claude-sonnet-5-5, used only for what a script cannot check: whether key facts are present and correct, whether a misconception is asserted, the fit to the learner's level, and whether a tutor reply did what its probe expected. After lecture 24 you check it against ten labels of your own, in section 10 of EVAL.md. See The bench: scenarios, scores and cost.

key

An answer key, keys/NNNN.json: for each quiz item the answer, its accepted forms, a rationale, and the misconception behind each wrong option. Every scenario's workspace holds a quiz's key, which is why your guard keeps keys/ from the main conversation. See Your tutor, configs and snapshots.

Not to be confused with a blind comparison's key file, bench/compare/<session-id>.key.json, which holds the secret that says which config was on which side. Nobody opens it before the reveal: not you, not the Guide, not the Builder. See The blind comparison.

Kind

In a round's What changed, the kind of change: modified skill, new skill, new subagent or other, such as an allow rule. No round in c1-skills may be new subagent. In the inventory, Kind is skill or subagent. See Your reviews.

ladder, the

The fixed sequence of configs you build and measure: c0-baseline, c1-skills, c2-review, c3-split and final, plus at least one final-minus-<x>. Each step is measured against the one before it, in quality and in cost. See Start here.

learning session

A real session of yours, learning something you do not know yet with final, on one of your test topics. You hold three, all on the same topic, and write each up in reviews/final.md under "Learning sessions", with a Date line and a Topic line that starts with the topic's id; they are the live tries of final's major review. See final, under the learning sessions.

length ratio

teach_length_ratio: the lesson's visible words divided by 150 words a minute, over the minutes requested. From 0.6 to 1.5 counts as on length; it is a rough estimate, so treat it as a warning light and not a verdict. See The bench: scenarios, scores and cost.

list-price equivalent

The dollar figure Claude Code reports for a session, shown in run.json and the reports. It is what the tokens would cost at list price, not what you pay on a subscription; on your plan, the cost is your usage limit. See The bench: scenarios, scores and cost.

live run

A run of --config live: tutor/ as it stands, with no snapshot, written under bench/runs/_live/, which git ignores. Quick rounds use live runs; they are never committed or compared, so quote what you saw in your thoughts. See Your reviews.

live tries

Your own use of the tutor for a major review, in a session started in tutor/: asking for a short lesson, taking a quiz, having your answers graded, asking it a question. You write them under **Live tries:**; for final, your three learning sessions are its live tries. See Your reviews.

major review

The review that closes a step, after its snapshot and its full run: that run's outputs and your live tries, criterion by criterion, each rated from 1 to 5 with feedback and evidence, then how the step compares with the one before and what the next step should fix. There are four, for c1-skills, c2-review, c3-split and final. See Your reviews.

marker line

A line in a template file, holding TODO, where your words go. p3 check lists a part that still holds one as work to do, and p3 check --final fails on it; a marker word inside code you quote is your quote, not a marker. See Your reviews.

misconception

A wrong belief real learners hold, listed in a topic with an id, its text and its correction; the shared topic has six. A key ties each wrong option to one, a grade names the one behind a wrong answer, and the judge checks that a lesson never asserts one. See The bench: scenarios, scores and cost.

objective

One thing a learner can explain or do, with an id such as O3. The shared topic has five, from O1 the agent loop to O5 multi-agent systems; the bench teaches O3, quizzes on O4, and grades quiz 0001, which covers O1 and O2. A lesson declares its objective in its p3-objective meta tag. See The bench: scenarios, scores and cost.

p3 check

The checklist of your repo, in two sections: Integrity, where anything broken is a FAIL, and Hand-in, where work still to do is a TODO line. It reads your reviews too, and makes no model call. CI runs it on every push; with --final, every TODO becomes a FAIL, which makes it the last check before you hand in. See Commands and files.

p3 next

Prints where you are on the project's map and the one next thing to do, in lines headed Done, Now, Next, Read (the guide page and section that explains it) and sometimes Also. It reads the state p3 check reads, makes no model call and changes nothing; the Guide runs it at the start of every session. See Commands and files.

pass

One revise-and-review iteration inside one of your tutor's review loops, so a loop capped at two passes reviews a draft at most twice. "Pass" never means a quick round. See Step 2: review loops.

plain terminal

A terminal tab in work/p3-tutor with no Claude Code in it (Git Bash on Windows). Full bench runs go there, because they take long and must not stop when a Claude Code window closes, and so do your blind comparisons, because the picks must be yours. See The Guide and the Builder.

PREFERENCES.md

tutor/PREFERENCES.md: how you like to learn, under five fixed headings, in your own words. It is part of every config and is what makes your reviews and your blind picks personal; c0-baseline keeps the neutral one the template ships, and c1-skills is the first config with yours. See Write your PREFERENCES.md.

PREREG.md

The length study's pre-registration: your hypothesis, the smallest difference that matters, your measures and your analysis rule, written in sections 1 to 4 and committed before the first of the study's eighteen runs, which git history proves. For 598E and the length-study stretch only. See Stretch options and the 598E length study.

probe

One tutor scenario: a single learner message sent through /tutor, such as a request for a quiz answer or a question far above the learner's level. The shared topic has six, tutor-p01 to tutor-p06; each has an expected behavior the judge checks, and some a leak check on a quiz item. See The bench: scenarios, scores and cost.

quick round

How you build a step: run the tutor, read and try what it made, write a few sentences under a round's **My thoughts:** in reviews/<step>.md, and ask the Builder to act on it; it makes one change and writes the round's What changed. p3 check counts at least two complete rounds in each of c1-skills, c2-review and c3-split; with one new component per round, the review loops on teach, quiz and grade take at least three rounds in c2-review. See Your reviews.

quick run

A run made with --quick: teach, quiz, grade-s1, tutor-p01 and tutor-p02, once. It is for trying things out, so p3 report and p3 compare leave quick runs out unless you add --quick, and p3 check never counts them. See Run the baseline and read what it made.

rating

A whole number from 1 (failing) to 5 (excellent) for one criterion of a major review, written as Rating: 4 on its own line with the feedback that justifies it below. The scale means the same at every step, so p3 report can set your ratings side by side. See Your reviews.

repeat

One of the runs that --repeats N makes, one after another, each with its own run id ending in -r1, -r2 and so on. c0-baseline and final get three repeats, so you can see how far runs of the same config spread. See The bench: scenarios, scores and cost.

reveal

p3 compare --reveal <session-id>, run once every pair of a session has a pick. It prints the tally, the sign-test p-value, how often you could tell, your reasons grouped by the config that won, the objective checks for the job, and only then the cost. See The blind comparison.

run id

The name of a run's folder, made of the time, the topic, the minutes and the repeat, such as 20261105-141502-agent-architecture-m30-r1. A run of c1-skills lives in bench/runs/c1-skills/<run-id>/. See The bench: scenarios, scores and cost.

Run line

The **Run:** line at the top of each quick round and major review, naming the run folder you read. A major review's must name a scored full run of its step on the shared topic. See Your reviews.

scenario

One headless Claude Code session, claude -p, in a fresh folder outside your repo that holds the config, the topic's starting workspace and FORMATS.md, with one request and its own time and turn limit: teach, quiz, one grade per submission and one tutor per probe. See The bench: scenarios, scores and cost.

shared topic, the

agent-architecture, in bench/topics/agent-architecture/: a generic introduction to agent architecture, written by me for a fixed novice, so my knowledge is the ground truth. You tune your tutor on it and only on it, and every blind comparison is made on it. See The bench: scenarios, scores and cost.

sign test

How the reveal reads a tally: ties set aside, it asks how often one config would win that many of the rest by chance if the two were equally good. With 10 non-tie picks, 8 for one config gives a two-sided p-value of about 0.11 and 9 about 0.02; with three pairs, even 3 of 3 gives 0.25. See The blind comparison.

skill

A SKILL.md in tutor/.claude/skills/<name>/: instructions your tutor follows when a job starts with /<name>, receiving the rest of the request as its arguments. The four names teach, tutor, quiz and grade are fixed so the bench can start each job; a config without one of them routes that job to /teach. See Your tutor, configs and snapshots.

snapshot

A copy of the live config in configs/<name>/, made by p3 snapshot <name>, with a hash of its files in SNAPSHOT.json, so a run's numbers belong to one exact tutor. A snapshot is frozen once it has runs: overwriting it makes p3 check fail, so make a new name instead. See Your tutor, configs and snapshots.

solver

One of the two models, claude-sonnet-5-5 and claude-haiku-5-5, that answer your quiz without the key. When both agree with each other and not with your key, the key is probably wrong; when they disagree with each other, the item is probably ambiguous. See The bench: scenarios, scores and cost.

stretch

25 of the 150 points: one of five options for 498E, and the length study for 598E, named by its id in the stretch field of p3.json and written up in section 13 of EVAL.md. In this project an A needs one. See Stretch options and the 598E length study.

subagent

A separate Claude with its own system prompt, its own tools and a fresh context, defined in a Markdown file in tutor/.claude/agents/ whose frontmatter has at least a name and a description. The main conversation starts one with the Agent tool and gets back one report; the subagent never sees the main conversation's history. See Your tutor, configs and snapshots.

tally

The wins for each config and the ties in one blind comparison session, the first thing the reveal prints. See The blind comparison.

teach-only run

A run made with --only teach, which is how the length study makes its eighteen runs at 10, 30 and 60 minutes. The --teach-only option shows these runs in p3 report and pairs them in p3 compare; they never count as a full run. See Stretch options and the 598E length study.

test topic

One of the two topics you write yourself in bench/own/<id>/, one from another Mines course and one life skill, in the same shape as the shared topic. They are the held-out check: you commit their items before any run, run c0-baseline and final once each on both, and review the results openly. See Your two test topics.

trace

<scenario>/trace.jsonl.gz in a run folder: the whole stream of the session, gzipped, including every subagent's messages. It shows where a scenario spent its turns, and whether your guard really ran in the bench. See The bench: scenarios, scores and cost.

trust question

"Quick safety check: Is this a project you created or one you trust?", which Claude Code asks the first time it starts in a folder. The highlighted choice is "No, exit", so press the down arrow to "Yes, I trust this folder", then Enter. See The Guide and the Builder.

tutor

What you build: the Claude Code configuration in tutor/ that does four jobs, teach, tutor, quiz and grade. "Your tutor" also means a session started with claude in work/p3-tutor/tutor, where you learn with it. The tutor job, one of the four, is the conversational helper, started with /tutor. See Your tutor, configs and snapshots.

workspace

The learner's folder a tutor works in. In the bench, each scenario's fresh folder holds the config, FORMATS.md and a copy of the topic's workspace/ (MISSION.md, NOTES.md, its learning records, quiz 0001 with its key, and the submissions), and nothing else from your repo. In your own sessions, tutor/ itself is the learner's workspace. See The bench: scenarios, scores and cost.

Your ratings

The table p3 report prints from the ratings in your major reviews, one row per criterion and one column per step, beside the objective checks and the cost; p3 report --reviews prints it alone, for EVAL.md. See Your reviews.

A term that is missing

If you meet a word in this guide, the README or a p3 message that is not here and not explained where you met it, ask the Guide what it means, and tell me, so I can add it.
