Part II · How it works

Your reviews

Your reviews are the part of P3 that only you can do. This page says what you write, exactly where you type it, how a quick round moves between your windows, how a major review and its ratings work, and how I grade them.

Why your reviews are the core of P3

A script can count the facts in a lesson and check a quiz key against two solvers. It cannot tell whether a lesson teaches you, whether a quiz item can be read two ways, whether feedback helps you fix a mistake, or whether the tutor is good to talk to. Much of a tutor's quality is personal fit, and you are the only person who can judge fit for you, against the tutor/PREFERENCES.md you wrote. So at every step you read and try what your tutor made, you write down what you think, and the next change to the tutor is built from what you wrote.

That gives your reviews three jobs, and each one is a reason to take them seriously.

  • They are the measurement. For everything a script cannot check, your judgment is the number. That is also why no agent writes them for you: an agent's words in their place would measure the agent, not your tutor.
  • They drive the build. Each quick round's thoughts become one change to the tutor, and what your c1-skills major review finds becomes the checklists of your reviewer subagents in c2-review.
  • They are the largest part of your grade. The reviews are 30 of the 150 points, the largest single part of the project, and the component inventory, whose reviews are also yours, is another 10.

When I read them, I look for a trail: from a review, to the change it caused, to whether that change helped. A step that made your tutor worse is a fine result to report, as long as the trail shows it.

The files you write in

All your reviews live in the reviews/ folder of your repo, and every file there is part of what you hand in.

Each file ships with its headings and with marker lines, which are lines that say TODO where your words go. You replace each marker with your own writing, and you keep every heading exactly as it is, because p3 check finds your reviews by their headings. reviews/README.md holds the same rules in short, with an example of each part; this page explains them and why they exist.

This page covers the quick rounds and the major reviews. The learning sessions and the open review of the test topics, both in reviews/final.md, are explained on final: the guard, repeats and the ablation and Your two test topics, and the inventory on The inventory and EVAL.md.

A quick round, from run to change

A quick round is one turn of the loop you build each step with. You run your tutor as it stands, read and try what it made, write a few sentences of thoughts in the step's review file, and the Builder makes one change to the tutor from them. The next run shows whether the change helped. Each round takes about 30 to 45 minutes of your own time for the reading, the thoughts and the change, plus the run itself.

Three places take part: the Builder, which changes the tutor and runs the commands you ask for; a Plain terminal, for runs you start yourself; and VS Code's editor, where you write. The Guide and the Builder explains the windows and how to open them.

  1. Run the tutor as it stands. Builder or Plain terminal. --config live runs tutor/ as it is right now, with no snapshot, and writes the run to bench/runs/_live/<run-id>/, a folder git ignores. A scenario is one task the bench gives your tutor, such as writing a lesson or answering one probe, and --quick runs five of them once: teach, quiz, grade-s1, tutor-p01 and tutor-p02.

    Run in the Builder, or a plain terminal
    uv run p3 bench --config live --quick

    When a round is about one job, run only that job with --only, such as --only quiz, or a list such as --only grade-s1,tutor-p03. A round about the quiz needs no lesson, and the lesson is by far the most expensive scenario: a quick run of the baseline took about 4 minutes and 1.1 million tokens in my pilot, and a quick run of my review-loop config would be about 6 million, nearly all of it the teach scenario. If you ask the Builder, it tells you how many sessions the run starts and asks before it starts one.

    Say to the Builder
    Run the live tutor on the quiz scenario only: uv run p3 bench --config live --only quiz

  2. Read and try what it made. Open the run's folder under bench/runs/_live/. Open the lesson in teach/outputs/lessons/ in your browser, take the quiz in quiz/outputs/quizzes/ yourself before you look at its key in quiz/outputs/keys/, read the feedback in grade-s1/outputs/grades/, and read the tutor's replies in tutor-p01/result.json and tutor-p02/result.json. When the round is about the tutor job, also talk to it: open a terminal in work/p3-tutor/tutor and start claude there Your tutor. Run the baseline and read what it made shows how to open each of these files.

  3. Write your thoughts in the file. In VS Code's editor, open reviews/<step>.md, such as reviews/c1-skills.md. In the round you are on, fill in the Run line and write a few sentences under **My thoughts:**: first whether the last round's change helped, then what worked, what did not, and what you want changed. Quote what you saw, because live runs are not committed and your thoughts are the only record of them that I see. You do not need every criterion here; write about what you noticed. Save the file.

  4. Ask the Builder to act on the round.

    Say to the Builder
    Act on Round 2 of reviews/c1-skills.md.

    Use your own round number and step file. One sentence is enough, because your thoughts are already in the file.

    The Builder reads your thoughts and the round before them, and makes one change to tutor/: a modified skill, a new skill, a new subagent, or another change such as an allow rule. It replaces the round's What changed marker lines with the Kind, the Files and the Why, and when the change adds or changes a component it fills in that component's factual fields in reviews/components.md. Then it says what it changed, suggests the cheapest run that shows the change, and waits. If your thoughts asked for several changes, it makes the one that matters most and tells you what it left for a later round.

  5. The next round's run shows whether it helped. Add the next round to the file (Adding a round says how), run the changed tutor, and begin its thoughts by saying whether the last change helped. That first sentence is the review of the change, and it is the link in your trail that I look for most.

What p3 next says at each point of the loop

uv run p3 next reads your step's review file and tells you where you are in the loop. You can run it in a plain terminal, or ask the Guide, which runs it for you. These are the lines it gives during the rounds of a step, with your round number and step file in place of k and <step>.

The Next line of p3 next while you build a step in quick rounds.
Where you areWhat p3 next says
Round k is untouchedRun the live tutor (uv run p3 bench --config live --quick, or --only with the scenarios you are working on), then write what you noticed under Round k > My thoughts in reviews/<step>.md.
Your thoughts are written, and What changed still holds its markersIn the Builder window, say: Act on Round k of reviews/<step>.md.
Something else in the round is not finishedFinish Round k in reviews/<step>.md: <its first problem>.
Every round is complete, and you have fewer than the minimumAdd Round k to reviews/<step>.md by copying the last round with its marker lines.
The minimum is reached, in step 1, and one of the four required skills is missing from tutor/Add a round for the <job> skill: copy the last round of reviews/c1-skills.md with its marker lines, as Round k.
The minimum is reached otherwise<what the step must hold>, snapshot it: uv run p3 snapshot <step> (or add another round first).

The start of that last line names what the step must hold before its snapshot, which neither p3 next nor p3 check can see: "When the four skills are in and you are happy with the tutor" in step 1, "When teach, quiz and grade each have their review loop and you are happy with the tutor" in step 2, and "When the teach job is split across its subagents and you are happy with the tutor" in step 3.

Watch out

The rule that the Builder writes only What changed and never your words lives in the repo's CLAUDE.md. A Builder started in work/p3-tutor reads that file by itself when it starts, which is one reason the Builder always starts there. A session that has not read it does not know the rule, so ask to act on a round only in the Builder window, as reviews/README.md says, and never in a Claude Code session that started somewhere else, such as the course repo's root.

Exactly where you type

You type your reviews into the file, in VS Code's editor, and never into a chat window. In VS Code's Explorer (the list of files on the left), open the reviews folder and click the step's file, such as c1-skills.md. Write under the headings that are there, replacing the marker lines, and save.

Why it is this way

The file is what I read and grade, and what p3 check and p3 report read. A chat message is gone when its window closes. And if you type your thoughts into the Builder and ask it to put them in the file, the words in the file are the agent's, which measures the agent. If you want help with wording, ask for it in a chat, and then type your own version into the file.

A filled-in round

This is the example from reviews/README.md: a student's Round 2, with their thoughts and the Builder's What changed.

### Round 2

**Run:** bench/runs/_live/20261105-141502-agent-architecture-m30-r1, a quick run

**My thoughts:**

The new quiz skill helped: the six items now mix mcq, numeric and short, where the quiz in Round 1 was six mcq items.
But q4 asks for "the best design" without saying best for what, and I could argue for two of its options.
The feedback on s1 q5 marks it partial, which is right, but it only says "You are close, think about why", and it never says that my answer gave the what and missed the why.
I want feedback that says which part of an answer was right, which part was missing, and one thing to reread.

**What changed:**

- Kind: new skill
- Files: tutor/.claude/skills/grade/SKILL.md
- Why: Grading still ran inside /teach with no rule for feedback; a grade skill of its own names what was right, what was missing and one thing to reread for every item, as these thoughts ask.

Notice what makes these thoughts work. The first sentence reviews the last change, with evidence (the mix of item types against Round 1's six mcq items). Each problem names its item (q4, s1 q5) and quotes what is wrong. And the last sentence says what the student wants, which is what the Builder acts on. Notice also that the Builder made one change, the grade skill, and did not also rewrite q4's skill in the same round; q4 waits for a later round.

Adding a round

The template ships two rounds in each of c1-skills.md, c2-review.md and c3-split.md, and one optional round in final.md. From the third round on, you add each round yourself, when you write its thoughts. The Builder never adds a round for you.

  1. Copy the last round, from its ### Round heading down to its - Why: line.

  2. Paste it below that round, still above ## Major review, and change the number in the heading.

  3. Put the marker lines back, so that its Run, My thoughts, Kind, Files and Why all say TODO. Then write the Run line and your thoughts, and save.

The new round should look like this before you write in it:

### Round 3

**Run:** TODO

**My thoughts:**

TODO

**What changed:**

- Kind: TODO
- Files: TODO
- Why: TODO
Why it is this way

The marker lines are how everyone knows where a round stands. The Builder acts on the round whose What changed lines are still markers, and p3 next and p3 check tell an untouched round, a half-written one and a complete one apart by them. If you copy a finished round and keep its Kind, Files and Why, the new round looks complete: the Builder does not know it has work there, and your trail shows a change that never happened in that round.

Watch out

Once a step has its minimum of rounds, a round you never wrote in gets a warning from p3 check ("not written; fill it in or delete it"), so delete a round you added by mistake. The optional round in final.md is the exception: left untouched, it is ignored.

One component per round, and why

A component is a skill or a subagent of your tutor. Every round makes one change, and when that change adds a component, it adds exactly one. A round may instead change a component that already exists. The four kinds of change the What changed line can name are modified skill, new skill, new subagent and other, for a change that is none of these, such as an allow rule in tutor/.claude/settings.json.

The reason is evidence. The run at the start of a round shows your tutor without the new component, and the run at the start of the next round shows it with it, so the two Run lines and your next thoughts are a with-and-without comparison that costs you nothing extra. That comparison is exactly what the component inventory asks you for.

An example from step 2: how the rounds line up into a with-and-without for each reviewer.
RoundIts Run lineIts change
c2-review Round 1the tutor without any revieweradds a quiz-reviewer subagent
c2-review Round 2the tutor with quiz-reviewer; the thoughts begin by saying whether it helpedadds a grade reviewer
c2-review Round 3the tutor with the grade reviewer; the thoughts say whether it helpedadds a lesson reviewer

Without the rule, two components arrive in one round, and the next run shows both changes at once. You cannot say which one helped, which one cost the extra tokens, or whether one of them made things worse while the other made them better. Each of them then needs a separate with-and-without check for the inventory, which means extra runs and extra tokens.

The last round of a step has no next round. Its change is reviewed by the major review that follows it, because the step's full run is the tutor with that change.

How many rounds, and the c1-skills rule

  • At least two complete rounds in each of reviews/c1-skills.md, reviews/c2-review.md and reviews/c3-split.md. Rounds in reviews/final.md are optional; give one to each component that final adds, such as a grader subagent, so its inventory entry has its with and without.
  • A round is complete when your thoughts and the Kind, Files and Why lines are all written, and Kind is one of the four kinds. The Run line is not part of that count, but p3 check warns when a complete round has none, because a round without its run cannot show its evidence.
  • Two is a floor, not a plan. With one new component per round, step 1 usually takes four rounds or more: one for each new skill and one to rewrite teach. Step 2 takes at least three, one for the reviewer of each of teach, quiz and grade, even though p3 check counts only two; p3 next's advice before the c2-review snapshot says so. Across the three steps that is about nine rounds for 498E, and about fourteen for 598E, whose architecture is larger.

c1-skills is still one agent. No round in reviews/c1-skills.md adds a subagent: each one changes or adds a skill, or makes another change the skills need, such as an allow rule (Kind other). Subagents arrive in c2-review, as reviewers, and in c3-split, as the teach pipeline, so that each step on the ladder adds one kind of machinery and its measurement is about that kind. p3 check fails, even without --final, when configs/c1-skills/ holds a subagent or a round in reviews/c1-skills.md is of Kind new subagent:

Run in the Builder, or a plain terminal
uv run p3 check
  FAIL  c1-skills adds a subagent, but c1 is still one agent (subagents arrive in c2-review and c3-split)
          reviews/c1-skills.md, Quick rounds > Round 3: Kind is new subagent; a quick round of c1-skills changes or adds a skill, or makes another change (Kind other)

If your thoughts in step 1 ask for something that sounds like a reviewer, write it down for step 2. Those notes are exactly what your c2-review checklists are made of.

Major reviews

A major review closes each step: c1-skills, c2-review, c3-split and final. Where a quick round is a few sentences about what you noticed, a major review goes through every criterion of every job the step reviews, rates each one from 1 to 5, and justifies each rating with evidence. It is written at the end of the step's file, under ## Major review. In my estimate it takes about 2 hours for c1-skills, 1.75 for c2-review, 0.75 for c3-split and 1.5 for final.

When to write it

  1. Snapshot the step when you are happy with its rounds, such as uv run p3 snapshot c1-skills, in the Builder or a plain terminal. A snapshot is a frozen copy of your tutor's config in configs/<name>/, so a run's numbers belong to one exact tutor. Step 1 explains it.

  2. Start its full run in a plain terminal, such as uv run p3 bench --config c1-skills. A full run is all ten scenarios: in my pilot one took about 6 minutes for the baseline and about 17 for my review-loop config, at the default --jobs 1, and the teach scenario alone may run up to 45 minutes. That is longer than Claude Code lets a foreground command run, and the run must not stop when a Claude Code window closes, so it goes in a plain terminal; The bench: scenarios, scores and cost has the numbers.

  3. Try the tutor live while tutor/ still matches the snapshot. You can do this while the full run runs.

  4. Read the run's outputs and write the review, once the run has finished and been scored.

  5. Commit the review as soon as you finish it.

Why it is this way

A major review is a review of one exact run of one exact snapshot, so its Run line names that run, and p3 check checks that the review was first committed complete after the run finished. A review written before the run it names would be a review of something you had not seen.

Run in a plain terminal in work/p3-tutor
git add reviews/c1-skills.md
git commit -m "Major review of c1-skills"
git push

You can also ask the Builder to commit it for you, with the file and the message. At the end of each step you commit configs/ and bench/runs/ as well, so the snapshot and the run your review names are in your repo.

What you review

That run's outputs, all of them, for the jobs the step reviews. In bench/runs/c1-skills/<run-id>/ (with your own step and run id), that is:

  • the lesson in teach/outputs/lessons/, opened in your browser;
  • the quiz and its key in quiz/outputs/, in quizzes/ and keys/;
  • the grades in grade-s1/outputs/grades/ and grade-s2/outputs/grades/;
  • each tutor reply in tutor-pNN/result.json, for tutor-p01 to tutor-p06.

Your own live tries. Open a terminal in work/p3-tutor/tutor, start claude there Your tutor, and use the tutor as a learner: ask for a short lesson, take a quiz, have your answers graded, and ask the tutor a question. The topic is yours to choose. The shared topic makes it easy to set what you saw beside the run, and a topic you really want to learn tells you more about fit.

Say to your tutor
/teach a 15-minute lesson on how an agent decides when to stop

Any request a learner would make works; this one starts the teach skill on a short lesson.

The learner files these tries leave in tutor/ (MISSION.md, NOTES.md, lessons/ and the rest) stay there from step to step, as a real learner's would. They are never part of a config, so you need not clear them between steps.

Watch out

From c2-review on, your tutor waits for subagents, and in an interactive session it can do that only once fork mode, a Claude Code setting that is on by default, is off. Put {"env": {"CLAUDE_CODE_FORK_SUBAGENT": "0"}} in tutor/.claude/settings.local.json and start claude again in tutor/. That file is personal and never reaches the bench; the last section of tutor/.claude/agents/README.md explains why it must not go in tutor/.claude/settings.json.

One thing about the quiz in the bench: the quiz scenario's workspace holds no lesson, so there "the lesson's core" means the objective's text and what MISSION.md and NOTES.md say the learner needs. In your live tries, judge a quiz against the lesson it follows.

My advice for the blind comparison (Which fits you better) helps here too: on the shared topic you are not the novice the topic describes, so judge fit by whether the output would have worked for you when you were new to agents.

The rating scale

The scale from reviews/README.md, the same at every step.
RatingWhat it means
5excellent: nothing you would change
4good: small problems you can name
3acceptable: it works, with real problems
2poor: problems that would hurt a learner
1failing: wrong, unusable or missing

Use the same scale at every step, because p3 report puts your ratings side by side, one column per step, and a 4 should mean the same thing in c1-skills as in final. Write each rating as a whole number on its own line, Rating: 4, with the feedback below it. If you are torn between two numbers, pick one and say in the feedback what kept it from the other; a range such as 3-4, or 4.5, is not a rating p3 check accepts. A job that failed to produce anything, such as a missing lesson, is a 1 on its criteria, with the run's evidence that it is missing.

Evidence

Every rating needs feedback that justifies it, and feedback needs evidence that I can find:

  • a quote from the lesson, the quiz, the feedback or a reply;
  • an item id, such as q4, or a submission and item, such as s2 q4;
  • a probe id, such as tutor-p05;
  • a section of the lesson, by its heading.

"Rating: 4. Good." is not a review. This is what one criterion looks like when it is done well, from reviews/README.md:

#### Wording and possible misreadings

Rating: 3

Four of the six items read cleanly, but two do not.
q2 asks which design "is better" without saying better for what, and both (b) and (c) are defensible for a learner on a small budget.
q5's correct option is the only one that repeats a phrase from the stem ("ends the loop"), so it gives itself away.
The reviewer in c2 should check every stem for an unstated criterion and every correct option for words copied from its stem.

It says how much of the quiz is fine, names each problem by its item, quotes the words that cause it, explains why it hurts a learner, and ends with something a reviewer could check for. That last sentence is why the c1-skills review matters so much: what it finds becomes the checklists of your reviewers in c2-review, and a reviewer told "check that no stem can be read two ways, as q4 could in my c1 review" finds more than one told "review this quiz".

The two closing sections

Every major review ends with two sections of your own.

  • ### Compared with the previous step. Job by job, how this step compares with the one before it, using the table above. For c1-skills the previous step is c0-baseline, which has no major review of its own, so compare with what you read in your baseline runs. For final, the quiz and the feedback compare with c2-review and the tutor with c1-skills, because c3-split reviewed only the lesson.
  • ### What the next step should fix, or in final.md ### What I would do next. The problems you want the next step to solve, specifically enough to act on. The Builder reads this section when it starts the next step's rounds, so a vague line here becomes a vague next step.

The major review of final

The review of final covers all four jobs, on one of its three full runs. Its live tries are your three learning sessions with final on one of your test topics, written up under ## Learning sessions in the same file, so its Live tries line points at them and names anything else you tried; a line is enough. final: the guard, repeats and the ablation walks through it.

Watch out

Write the review of final and commit it before you start the blind comparison of c0-baseline against final. Your open judgment has to be on record before your blind one, because EVAL.md asks you to set the two side by side, and a review written after you saw the reveal is no longer an open review to compare with. The blind comparison explains why.

What p3 check says about your reviews, and the usual fixes

uv run p3 check reads every file in reviews/ and lists what is still to do in them. It makes no model call and changes nothing, so run it whenever you finish a round or a review, in the Builder or a plain terminal, or ask the Guide to run it. Each line starts with one of four words:

  • OK: the item holds.
  • TODO: work not done yet. It never fails the check on its own, but p3 check --final, which you run before you hand in, fails on every one of them.
  • WARN: worth a look, never a failure.
  • FAIL: something is broken.

Under a line, indented, it names the file's part with its headings, as in Major review > Lesson > Pedagogy. A round in progress looks like this:

Run in the Builder, or a plain terminal
uv run p3 check
  TODO  reviews/c1-skills.md, quick rounds: 1 of the 2 complete rounds needed
          Quick rounds > Round 2: Kind is still TODO
          Quick rounds > Round 2: Files is still TODO
          Quick rounds > Round 2: Why is still TODO

That one is not a problem: it means your Round 2 thoughts are written and the Builder has not acted on them yet. The table below lists the messages you are most likely to see and what to do about each.

Note

UTF-8 and Windows PowerShell. VS Code saves files as UTF-8 by default, so a review you type in the editor is always fine. A file written by a command can differ: in Windows PowerShell, > and Out-File write UTF-16, which p3 check cannot read. To fix it, look at the bottom right of the VS Code window, where the status bar shows the file's encoding, such as "UTF-16 LE"; click it, choose "Save with Encoding", and pick "UTF-8". If the text looks garbled, choose "Reopen with Encoding" first and pick the encoding the file really has, then save it as UTF-8.

Note

A TODO in your quotes is not a marker. If you quote code that says TODO, such as the to-do comment in a lesson's exercise, put it in backticks or a code block, and p3 check treats it as your quote.
